Trend Following and Mean Reversion ETF Portfolios

Historical OOS and conclusions

Study endpoint 15 October 2026 · Report prepared 19 October 2026
Scored period 2024-01-01 to 2026-10-15

Executive summary

We compare four unchanged allocations across six fixed long/cash ETF strategies. At baseline costs, the historical daily-cap allocation has cash-excess Sharpe 2.481, CAGR 21.85% and daily maximum drawdown -4.24%. Cost-matched passive QQQ has Sharpe 0.996, CAGR 23.93% and drawdown -22.77%.

Retrospective evidence only. Configurations, ETF pruning and portfolio weights were chosen using the already examined 2024–2026 sample. Applying those weights backward does not create independent portfolio development or validation. The historical OOS label describes the parent strategy window, not an untouched portfolio test. This distinction matters after extensive candidate and allocation search [4].

1 Project goal

Our objective is to study whether combining causal trend following and oversold-recovery strategies can improve the return and drawdown profile relative to passive ETF exposure, after explicit execution costs. This is a reproducible historical research project, not a trading recommendation.

We ask how the same four static initial allocations behave in different periods, how costs change the result, how much return remains after passive-ETF attribution, and whether the historical capital-share constraint survives backward comparison.

2 Research outline

Trend persistence motivates the trend hypothesis [1], but our ETF implementation is not a replication of that paper. The parent study evaluated 379 definitions per ETF: 289 trend-following and 90 long/cash mean-reversion configurations. Independent development and validation qualification used a strict joint gate at 25-bp slippage: cash-excess Sharpe above matched own-ETF buy-and-hold and CAGR above two-thirds of its CAGR, except that a negative benchmark CAGR requires positive candidate CAGR. Nonfinite candidates cannot pass. Later ETF filtering and correlation pruning led to these six sleeves.

We retain four comparisons: a historical daily-cap Sharpe fit, an initial-only capped fit, an uncapped Sharpe fit and equal initial capital. We exclude the standalone maximum-CAGR 100%-GDX strategy allocation; GDX remains an underlying sleeve. These allocations are saved historical fits, not newly optimized solutions or global-optimum certificates.

3 Data

The parent universe spans 56 US-listed equity-index, international, sector, industry, bond and commodity ETFs. This portfolio report uses GDX, FXI, QQQ, UNG, FDN and BND. Data came from Alpha Vantage daily adjusted records and observed 30-minute bars: native raw captures where available, and matching-vintage-restored legacy captures otherwise. Daily anchors and available 15-minute observations provide same-provider reconciliation checks, not independent-vendor confirmation. The study does not establish point-in-time vendor availability. Daily feature history begins in 2015 where available.

4 Backtesting

Indicators are causal and daily, observed with native half-hour checks. Entry and exit require the shared three-trading-hour confirmation, equivalent to seven consecutive half-hour checks, followed by execution at the next eligible observed bar open. Each stage starts flat with $1,000; only feature history is carried, not holdings, orders or confirmation streaks. Accounts are unlevered, fractional long/cash, with the parent study’s corporate-action, dividend/reinvestment and paid terminal-liquidation conventions preserved.

Cash accrues at 3% nominal ACT/365, event-compounded. It is neither downloaded Treasury return nor a 3% APY assumption. We do not splice stages into a single wealth curve or manufacture combined trade counts from six independent accounts.

Vc,t = Σi wiEc,i,t   and   rc,t = [Vc,t / Vc,t−1] − 1

Here c identifies the cost scenario; Ec,i,0 = 1, Vc,0 = 1 and Σiwi = 1. Each Ec,i,t is saved sleeve wealth normalized to its initial account. Static initial wealth mixing is not constant-weight daily return averaging. The same exact weights apply to every cost and period, with no cross-sleeve cash funding or rebalance trades.

Metric definitions

Cash-excess Sharpe is √252 times the mean daily return minus matched cash return, divided by its sample standard deviation with one degree-of-freedom correction. It does not mean outperformance relative to buy-and-hold [2]. Square-root-252 annualization is descriptive and does not supply dependence-robust statistical inference [3]. CAGR uses final normalized wealth and the actual initial-to-terminal ACT/365 clock. Volatility is the annualized sample standard deviation of net daily returns. Drawdown uses daily valuations and includes the initial balance; it is not the worst intraday loss. Matched cash has undefined Sharpe because its cash-excess variance is zero.

Attribution definitions

We fit saved net cash-excess portfolio returns on passive QQQ cash-excess returns with an intercept, and separately on all six cost-matched passive ETF cash-excess returns jointly. The factors are passive buy-and-hold accounts, not active strategy returns. Annual arithmetic alpha is 252 times the daily intercept, not CAGR. Betas are conditional linear sensitivities, not portfolio weights; negative beta does not imply an executed short. R² is descriptive, with no significance or causal-skill claim.

5 Execution and slippage

Every purchase and sale pays 1-bp commission plus one-way slippage of 1, 3, 10 or 25 bps. The baseline is 3-bp slippage, or 4 bps total per side. No crypto-course fee assumptions are transferred to ETFs. Each scenario has its own cost-matched passive QQQ and cash control and retains the original paid liquidation. We do not change signals or weights when cost assumptions change.

7 Performance evaluation

Period interpretation

The uncapped fit has the highest baseline Sharpe but far lower CAGR than the other fitted allocations. The initial-only capped fit has higher CAGR than the daily-cap fit but does not enforce the required drifting-capital constraint. The daily-cap fit has lower CAGR and a smaller observed daily drawdown than passive QQQ. All of these comparisons are conditioned on examining this same historical sample.

For the current allocation, CAGR is 21.98% at 1-bp slippage and 20.50% at 25-bp slippage, with the additional 1-bp commission in both cases. No cost scenario triggers reselection or weight refitting.

Active strategy return correlations

This saved analysis uses the 700 historical OOS daily observations from 2024-01-02 through 2026-10-15 at baseline costs. It measures active net strategy returns, including flat and cash periods, not the passive ETF factors used in attribution.

QQQ and FDN strategy correlation is 0.649855; GDX and UNG is -0.063184. These are sample descriptions, not future diversification guarantees, risk limits or a paired long-short strategy test.

Conclusions and limitations

The four allocations expose a genuine research trade-off: a high fitted Sharpe need not maximize compound growth, and apparent diversification can partly reflect cash-heavy sleeves rather than independent alpha. Comparisons across periods and costs are descriptive robustness checks. We do not select a new winner, alter parent-stage membership, refit weights, or claim the examined OOS sample became untouched again.

The assignment’s momentum and reversal themes are addressed through indicator-window trend and oversold-recovery rules, cost sensitivity, allocation comparisons and passive-factor attribution. News/activity filters, seasonal effects, mechanical rebalancing events, liquidity-driven reversals, pairs/spreads and macro-regime-conditioned signals were not separately tested. Those are future extensions, not findings of this report. This long/cash study is not market-neutral statistical arbitrage.

A prospective next step would require a new preregistered release, frozen identities and weights, explicit future evaluation dates, and a practical costed policy if capital-share limits are to be enforced. Nothing in this report authorizes live trading.

Audit and reproducibility

The report is generated only from hash-bound saved aggregate inputs. Companion CSV/JSON retain full precision for all 16 portfolio, 8 control, 16 cap, 16 single-factor and 16 joint-factor rows in this stage. The three reports do not pool returns or substitute another period’s clock. Public verification checks source/output hashes, identities, costs, stage boundaries, exact saved weights and chart endpoints; reproducing original trades still requires the private parent-study inputs and engine.

The earlier study uses development 2017–2020, validation 2021–2023 and historical OOS 2024–2026-10-15. A separately preserved corrected-split experiment is inactive and is neither merged into these reports nor used for selection.
